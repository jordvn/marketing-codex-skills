# Marketing Action Brief

## Overview

Use this skill to convert messy marketing evidence into a clear brief a team can act on. Optimize for decision clarity, prioritization, owners, sequencing, and communication that executives and cross-functional partners can absorb quickly.

## Core Workflow

1. Identify the audience, decision, deadline, business goal, and desired deliverable: brief, deck outline, 30/60/90 plan, decision memo, or action matrix.
2. Inventory the source material and separate evidence, interpretation, open questions, and recommendations.
3. Read `references/brief-formats.md` to choose the appropriate output shape.
4. Prioritize actions by impact, urgency, confidence, effort, dependencies, and reversibility.
5. Translate findings into owner-ready next steps with clear outcomes and validation checks.
6. Make uncertainty explicit. Do not smooth over tracking issues, small samples, source conflicts, or missing context.

## Output Formats

Choose the format requested by the user. If no format is specified, return:

1. **Executive Summary**: decision, recommendation, and why now.
2. **What We Know**: strongest evidence and confidence.
3. **What Needs Action**: prioritized actions with owner, timing, expected outcome, and validation.
4. **Open Questions**: checks needed before larger commitments.
5. **Suggested Narrative**: optional presentation or stakeholder talking points.

## Briefing Principles

- Lead with the decision or action, not the analysis journey.
- Preserve the evidence trail: cite URLs, metrics, campaigns, segments, page elements, reports, or assumptions when available.
- Convert vague recommendations into verbs: fix, test, refresh, monitor, validate, pause, scale, brief, or assign.
- Separate "do now" actions from "investigate before deciding" checks.
- Keep presentation outlines slide-ready, with one main point per slide.
- Use confidence labels when recommendations depend on incomplete or questionable data.

If the user requests a deck, provide a slide-by-slide outline unless they explicitly ask for a generated slide file.
