# Report Readiness

Use this reference before presenting conclusions, recommendations, dashboard feedback, or executive summaries.

## Minimum Context To Check

- Reporting period and comparison period
- Whether current period is complete
- Data source and export date
- Metric definitions and event/key event names
- Filters, segments, channel grouping, and exclusions
- Known launches, site releases, GTM publishes, consent changes, campaign changes, and CRM rule changes
- Sample size and denominator for rates
- Business goal: lead volume, qualified pipeline, SEO growth, campaign learning, launch QA, or executive reporting

## Confidence Labels

- **High confidence**: multiple sources align, sample is sufficient, period is complete, definitions are clear, and likely confounders are checked.
- **Medium confidence**: pattern is plausible but one or two validation checks are missing.
- **Low confidence**: small sample, incomplete period, missing definitions, source mismatch, or tracking concerns could change the conclusion.
- **Unsafe to conclude**: data-quality issue likely invalidates the metric or comparison.

## Common Reporting Risks

- Comparing a complete prior period to an incomplete current period.
- Treating raw leads as success without qualification or pipeline context.
- Treating last-click attribution as the full buyer journey.
- Mixing branded and non-branded SEO.
- Mixing traffic growth with demand quality.
- Ignoring traffic mix changes when interpreting conversion rate.
- Presenting correlation as causation without launch, budget, seasonality, or tracking checks.
- Overreacting to tiny samples or one-day movement.
- Hiding key exclusions in dashboard filters.
- Using vanity metrics without a decision attached.

## Recommended Finding Shape

Use this structure for each material finding:

```text
[Severity] Finding title
Evidence: metric movement, segment, source, URL/query/campaign, and period.
Likely explanation: tracking issue, real performance change, or opportunity.
Confidence: high/medium/low and why.
Next check: the exact validation step.
Action: fix, investigate, monitor, test, or scale.
```

## Executive Summary Shape

Keep the summary short and decision-oriented:

1. Whether the data is trustworthy enough to use.
2. The most important performance movement.
3. The most important tracking or data-quality concern.
4. The clearest opportunity.
5. The recommended next action and owner.

Avoid false precision. If the data is not strong enough for a recommendation, say what must be checked first.
