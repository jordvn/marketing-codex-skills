# Conversion Friction Patterns

Use these patterns when reviewing B2B SaaS landing pages, form flows, CTA paths, conversion reports, or page screenshots.

## Message Match

- Source promise and landing-page headline should align. Paid ad, email, partner, or organic intent mismatch can depress conversion even when page UX looks fine.
- Hero copy should state what the offer/product is, who it is for, and why the visitor should care.
- Generic claims need specifics: audience, use case, outcome, category, integration, industry, or measurable proof.
- If the page targets multiple personas, check whether the primary CTA and proof still feel coherent.

## CTA And Offer

- CTA text should describe the action or outcome, not only "Submit" or "Learn more."
- Primary and secondary CTAs should not compete for the same attention unless they represent meaningful commitment levels.
- Demo, trial, consultation, download, webinar, and assessment offers need different proof and form expectations.
- Weak offer alignment appears when high-intent visitors are sent to low-commitment content, or early-stage visitors are pushed directly to sales.

## Trust And Proof

- Add proof near points of anxiety: customer logos, quantified outcomes, testimonials, security/privacy cues, screenshots, integrations, awards, analyst references, or methodology.
- B2B buyers often need role, industry, company size, and use-case proof.
- Claims like "easy", "powerful", "AI-powered", or "enterprise-grade" should be backed by specifics.
- Comparison and alternative pages should be fair, specific, and credible.

## Form And Funnel Friction

- Review field count, required fields, validation, hidden fields, enrichment, calendar routing, privacy copy, and confirmation state.
- Multi-step forms should make progress and expectations clear.
- Gated content forms should balance lead quality with offer value.
- Calendar booking failures, routing delays, iframe issues, and thank-you page tracking can make conversion analysis misleading.

## Mobile And Layout

- Check whether the value proposition, proof, and CTA appear before excessive scrolling on mobile.
- Sticky CTAs can help mobile if they do not cover form fields or content.
- Long hero sections, dense navigation, oversized imagery, and buried proof can reduce scanability.
- Visual hierarchy should make the next action obvious without hiding important qualification context.

## Experiment Backlog Shape

```text
Hypothesis: If we change [element] for [audience/traffic source], then [metric] will improve because [reason].
Change: exact copy, layout, form, proof, or offer change.
Primary metric: conversion event.
Guardrail: lead quality, MQL rate, pipeline, bounce/engagement, or sales acceptance.
Segment: traffic source, device, persona, campaign, or landing page.
Confidence: high/medium/low and why.
```
