# SEO/AEO Opportunity Patterns

Use these patterns when reviewing Search Console, rank tracking, SERP notes, content inventories, page copy, or SEO sections of marketing reports.

## SEO Opportunity Signals

- High impressions with low CTR: improve title, meta description, SERP promise, brand clarity, rich result eligibility, and intent match.
- Average position 4-10 with meaningful impressions: refresh content, strengthen entity coverage, add proof, improve internal links, and sharpen the opening answer.
- Average position 11-20 with relevant impressions: expand topical depth, create supporting pages, consolidate overlap, or improve hub/category architecture.
- Clicks down while impressions are stable: investigate SERP layout, title/meta changes, brand demand, snippet relevance, and competitor changes.
- Impressions up while clicks and conversions lag: query mix may be broadening into weaker intent; split by branded, non-branded, commercial, and informational queries.
- Page has conversions but weak visibility: prioritize internal links, related content, schema, title/meta, and distribution.
- Page has visibility but weak conversions: improve CTA match, proof, product pathways, comparison blocks, and offer relevance.

## AEO/GEO Readiness Signals

- Queries with "what is", "how to", "best", "vs", "alternatives", "pricing", "examples", or "for [role/industry]" often need concise answer blocks.
- Pages should make entity relationships explicit: product, category, audience, use case, alternatives, integrations, proof, and differentiators.
- Add citation-worthy details: original data, named customer proof, methodology, definitions, comparison tables, expert quotes, and dated updates.
- Use FAQ sections only when questions are real and answerable; avoid filler Q&A.
- Prefer clear headings that map to buyer questions and AI answer extraction.
- Add schema when it reflects visible page content: Organization, Product, SoftwareApplication, FAQPage, Article, BreadcrumbList, Review, or HowTo where appropriate.

## Query/Page Mapping

- One primary page should own each important intent cluster.
- Product or solution pages should own commercial and category-fit queries.
- Educational articles should own problem, definition, and how-to queries, then route to relevant product pathways.
- Comparison pages should own "vs", "alternatives", "best", and category shortlist queries when the company can support them honestly.
- If multiple URLs rank for the same cluster, compare intent, content quality, internal links, canonical signals, and conversion path before consolidating.

## Prioritization Heuristics

- High impact: topics tied to pipeline, qualified demos, product categories, competitive displacement, or high-intent buyer education.
- High confidence: multiple sources align, volume is meaningful, page intent is clear, and the required fix is specific.
- Quick wins: CTR improvements, answer blocks, internal links, FAQ/comparison additions, schema cleanup, and refreshing near-ranking pages.
- Bigger bets: new hubs, category pages, comparison programs, research assets, and technical/indexation fixes.

## Recommended Finding Shape

```text
[Priority] Opportunity title
Evidence: query/page/metric/SERP/content signal.
Why it matters: business or audience implication.
Recommendation: specific page, section, schema, link, or content change.
Confidence: high/medium/low and why.
Next check: validation step before or after the change.
```
